My name is Hang Gao, a PhD student in Mechanical Engineering at Cornell University. I am advised by [Prof. E. Farrell Helbling](https://scholar.google.com/citations?user=Pwd7hJYAAAAJ&hl=en) and affiliated with the [Computer Systems Laboratory](https://www.csl.cornell.edu/). My current research focuses on the system-level modeling, design, model-based and learning-based control and estimation, and experimental validation of insect-scale flapping-wing robots operating under strict size, weight, power, and computation (SWPaC) constraints, where performance is fundamentally shaped by scaling laws.

I am leading the end-to-end development of **GammaBot**, an untethered flapping-wing robot weighing less than 1 gram, capable of high-speed water-surface locomotion, achieving 0.89 m/s forward speed, 400 deg/s turning, and over 10 minutes of endurance on a single charge.

Previously, I received my M.S. in Mechanical Engineering from the University of California San Diego, where I worked with [Prof. Nick Gravish](https://gravishlab.ucsd.edu/) on compliant thorax design for flapping-wing robots.
